SQ003 Reference-Design Exercise — "if I had to build this in 1999"

2026-07-26 overnight session. Method: design the product forward from its known constraints and parts, then use the design to predict the unknown topology of the real board. Every prediction is tagged DPn and is beep-checkable; none is asserted as fact until measured. Provenance of this whole file: [DS/inference], informed by the netlist (incl. the owner's stroke session) where noted.

1. The design brief E.G.O. faced

Control two "XtraLow" simmer positions on a pro gas range: read two knob pots, cycle two gas solenoids with a very low duty flame, gate the external 6-channel spark module during XLO ignition windows, pass through three other burners' spark requests, all from 120 VAC with a <10 VA budget, one-sided phenolic board, 1999 parts, cost-driven. Available: ST62T20 (OTP, ADC, triac-rated port A), 4N35, 48 V SPDT relay, 2 tab triacs, 1 SCR + 2.2 µF/400 V film cap, ~32 R / ~20 D / ~21 C / 9 small transistors.

2. Reference design, block by block

2.1 Power supply — transformerless, NEUTRAL-referenced (HYPOTHESIS, netlist-supported)

The owner's stroke session merged P5 (LINE-5V), P6 (NEUTRAL), and IC1 pin 1 (VDD) into one 566 mm² net [CV,H,M,V]. No direct P5↔P6 meter fact exists yet (BENCH_GUIDE A3/A3b are the test + falsifiers; rivals: line-referenced VDD, or a floating common reached only through high impedance). If it holds, this is the classic appliance topology:

NEUT (P6) ────────────┬──────────────────────── VDD  ("logic high" = neutral)
                      │ ZD (5.1V shunt)  + reservoir C (25V can, + to VDD)
LINE (P7) ──[C_drop]──┼──[R_inrush]──|<|──┬───── VSS  (−5 V below neutral)
                      │                   └── (2nd diode = return/clamp)
                      └—[R32 4.7k 2W]──|<|──[reservoir]── −48 V relay rail
  • Logic rail: series film-cap dropper off LINE, small inrush R, one rectifier + one return diode (or 4-diode bridge), reservoir electrolytic, 5.1 V shunt zener. CORRECTION (binding data): D12's anode sits DIRECTLY on LINE and ZD2 spans VDD<->N168 (not the VSS pour) — so the chain has two distinct sub-rail nodes (N254 rectified, N168 shunt-regulated) and the dropper's position is still open; the p1 schematic now draws the hidden chain as an unknown-order box. VDD is the NEUT pour; VSS is a separate −5 V pour — which explains the big unlabeled 243 mm² pour holding IC1 pin 20 [CV,H].
  • Relay rail: the coil needs 48 V @ 7.5 mA only while an XLO position is active. Cheapest: resistive dropper (R32, 2 W flameproof — 0.27 W actual, comfortable) off the rectified line into a small reservoir; a TO-92 (one of TR1..TR9) sinks the coil to VSS under MCU control. Freewheel diode across the coil.
  • Budget check (pipeline/psu_model.py): worst-case active load ≈ 25 mA. A single half-wave dropper would need ≥1.3 µF (implausible) → EITHER the rectifier is a bridge (≥0.64 µF works) OR the relay branch is fed separately through R32 (then the C-dropper only carries ≤16 mA → ≥0.4 µF full-wave). The presence of a dedicated fat 2 W resistor beside the relay strongly suggests the split branch.
  • Aging math (dimensionless): rail collapses when fractional C loss exceeds h/(1+h) for design headroom h — 23% loss kills a 30%-headroom design, and film X2 caps on 24/7 line duty routinely lose 10–50% by self-healing. This mechanism CLASS (supply collapse under load — dropper cap, reservoir ESR, or rectifier/zener drift are its members) reproduces all three observed facts: both XLO channels dying together, everything else still working, and a ~2-year repeat after a same-age refurb went in. Which member it is (if any) is exactly what bench steps B4a-B4c discriminate. (The failure window where the MCU still runs but the rail collapses under relay+gate load is wide.)

2.2 Pot interface

Rheostat mode: P5 (VDD potential) → knob pot → wiper P14/P15 → board-internal divider resistor to VSS → PB ADC pin. Ratiometric with VDD-VSS ✓ (ST62 ADC ref is the rails). Knob-off = spark-switch open is detected either as out-of-range ADC or via the switch line into the same network.

2.3 Request sensing (UNSEQ P1/P2/P3)

With logic already neutral-referenced, line-voltage switch signals need only a high-value flameproof series R + diode + clamp into a PB pin — no isolation needed. Predicts: D17/D18/D19 each in series with a large R (the R2/R7-class parts), summing or individually into PB inputs with zener/diode clamps.

2.4 Where the 4N35 actually goes

An opto in a fully line-referenced board is not needed for input sensing — it's needed where a signal must cross to a circuit referenced to the OTHER line pole or to the spark module's own domain. Best hypothesis: the MCU drives the 4N35 LED; the phototransistor (pins 4/5) closes the sequenced spark request loop to the external spark module (P8/P13 side), isolating the MCU domain from the spark module's flame-rectification-sensitive wiring. Alternative (weaker): flame/current sense input.

2.5 Solenoid + spark sequencing per XLO channel

  • T1/T2 = triacs (tab packages), gates via series R from PA pins 16–19 (datasheet direct-drive). Solenoid drive is HALF-WAVE through the triac (fires one polarity): 120 Vrms half-wave ≈ 54 V average (≈85 V rms incl. DC; a DMM reads somewhere in 50–85 V depending on coupling) — CONSISTENT with the field-service note "board outputs ~50 V AC to the solenoid," but only as a weak consistency check, not proof. One PA pin per channel for gas, one per channel for spark-window = 4 PA pins ✓.
  • The steering-diode rows beside T1/T2 (D15/D13/D10/D9 and D16/D14/D5/D6) split each channel's triac between the solenoid path (P9→…→P10/P11 RET) and the sequenced-spark path (P8/P13), per the handoff's multi-pad chain observation.
  • RET topology (Q4): OPEN. An earlier P10≡P11 common-return claim was withdrawn 2026-07-28 — owner audit showed three disjoint copper islands; the union was a terminal-sampling artifact (fixed: majority-comp-only unions). Bench A12 decides.
  • SCR1 + 2.2 µF/400 V + D1/D2: capacitor-discharge kick. Most plausible role: guarantee a crisp solenoid pull-in (charge the film cap off LINE, dump through the SCR into the valve coil at the moment of ignition), since a half-wave-fed solenoid alone can chatter at pull-in. Alternative: a sequencing pulse into the spark module. The 400 V film cap + SCR is overkill for logic, right-sized for a coil kick.

2.6 Relay (K1)

SPDT gating LINE onto SW LINE (P4) — the master enable for the XLO subsystem (protects against a stuck triac by de-energizing the whole branch when idle, and gives the "all other burners keep working" isolation). Driven by a TO-92 from a PA or PB pin to VSS.

2.7 MCU support

RES1 ceramic resonator on pins 3/4 with C10/C11 as its load caps (Q5 prediction); R+C on /RESET pin 7 (10 k + 10 nF class — one of the 103K100s); VPP pin 6 strapped to VSS; unused PB pins strapped (netlist already shows IC1.15 on VDD and IC1.12≡IC1.13 paired [H]); 100 nF-class decoupling at the IC corner; ZD4 (measured 2.5 V, K→VDD) most plausibly a threshold reference for a comparator-style sense (brown-out helper on RESET, or valve-current threshold) — its anode net is the tell.

The FMEA's top two both live in the PSU branch and are exactly what the powered ripple-under-load test discriminates — reinforcing the REPORT §5 ordering.

6. What changed in my thinking

  • The board being neutral-referenced with a negative VSS (if DP1 confirms) means every "5 V" probe point is still line-referenced — the bench safety rules in REPORT §5 stay mandatory, and scope grounds remain forbidden.
  • The relay's role as master XLO enable (SW LINE) raises the relay branch (rail, driver, contacts — not the coil) back up the list; the coil measuring healthy says little (codex finding #4 was right).
  • The 2.2 µF/SCR block being a valve pull-in kicker would put it in series with the solenoid event chain — a failed kicker = valves never pull in even with the MCU commanding correctly. That's testable: watch P9–P10 voltage at the commanded moment.
