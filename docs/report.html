4. Diagnosis — updated fault tree

What the symptom really localizes. Both XLO burners dead (no spark, no gas), all other burners fine, second occurrence in ~2 years. This localizes to shared XLO infrastructure, which is bigger than the board's PSU: pot excitation (P5) distribution, the pot/harness connectors, the relay-gated SW LINE path, solenoid returns, the board's MCU/PSU — and only field data narrows it: appliance techs report this exact both-burners-together failure as the classic simmer-board death, with the knob pots as a known co-failure. So the board remains suspect #1, but the range-side paths must be cleared cheaply before any board surgery is trusted.

Revised ranking (was: PSU → reset → osc → request path → link joint → MCU):

  1. Board fails the bench functional test (see §5). This is the highest-value single test, not an infallible world-splitter: a bench PASS with an unrealistic stimulus or load can miss a fault that only appears with real harness/solenoid/spark-module interaction or at cooktop temperature; a bench FAIL is only meaningful if the stimulus wiring is right (per the service-manual hookup). Interpret with those false-pass/false-fail modes in mind: pass ⇒ shift suspicion range-side (pots/harness/connectors — the documented co-failure); fail with clean rails ⇒ board fault, continue down this list.
  2. 5 V rail integrity under load — the SERIES dropper is C12 per the v3 netlist (NEUT → C12 → R10 390R → rail); C7 is the X2 line filter across LINE↔NEUT — its aging does not sag the rail. Dropper caps age; but a DMM average of 5 V is NOT sufficient: check AC ripple on VDD and reset-pin stability (brown-out / watchdog cycling can look like "5 V but dead").
  3. Relay path under load — coil resistance cleared the coil only. Contacts (P7→P4 SW LINE) can be burned/high-resistance; test closure and voltage drop while driving a real load.
  4. Solder joints / prior rework — NEW: IMG_1089 shows a solder lump on P10's base and a flux halo at P11 (the solenoid returns) — evidence this board was hand-reworked before. Inspect/reflow those and the link joints (single-sided phenolic + 24/7 heat = joint fatigue); do the P5-chain flex test from the handoff.
  5. Request-sense path — 4N35 LED (unpowered diode test, expect ~1.0–1.2 V across pins 1–2), D17–D20, dropping resistors.
  6. ZD4 — not fully exonerated (red-team): a 2.5 V reverse reading through surrounding circuitry is consistent with a reference but also with leakage. One powered measurement across it settles it. Keep on the list, below the above.
  7. Oscillator (RES1 + load caps; note the LFAO backup only masks a dead resonator if the OSG option bit is programmed — unknown for this firmware).
  8. MCU dead — last resort; OTP read-protected ⇒ donor board. (For completeness: ST62 protection is defeatable by specialist labs, not by DIY.)

Why "~2-year life ⇒ overstressed shunt PSU" was demoted: plausible, but unproven and it under-weights external stressors (relay arcing, marginal solenoid load, heat-cycled joints, line surges, or simply a refurb board with same-age parts). If an external load is killing boards, uprating PSU parts won't prevent failure #3 — so the repair should include the §6 hardening and the range-side checks regardless of root cause.

5. Owner bench checklist (ranked by information per minute)

Sequencing rationale: the board is currently OUT of the range on the bench, so board checks come first as a practical matter — but the range-side checks in step 5 are the other half of the diagnosis and should happen on the next visit to the range, before buying any parts, whichever order the visits happen in.

Unpowered (do first, 20 min total): 1. Q8 orientation beeps: IC1 pin 6→pin 20 ≈0 Ω and pin 20→P6-side common ≈0 Ω (locks pin numbering; everything downstream depends on it). 2. 4N35 LED: diode test across IC3 pins 1–2, expect ~1.0–1.2 V (its LED is the request-sense front end). 3. IC1 20-beep session (~15 min): COM = pin 20; beep every IC1 pin vs P5, P14, P15, RES1 legs, 4N35 pins 4/5, relay coil pads, T1/T2 gate pins, TR bases. Priorities inside the session (verification findings): (a) adjacent-pin beeps along pins 16–20 — that row is one fused blob in the copper imagery, so their separation is currently model-assumed, and pins 16–19 are the PA triac-drive outputs; (b) the two ends of link LK2 (just below pin 20 — its snap was ambiguous); (c) P2 and P3 each to their own diode pad in the D17/18/19 cluster (glare seams broke those runs in CV — two beeps bind them). Record in measurements.yaml — this + the netlist completes the logic map and falsifies/confirms every [V] link merge. 4. Inspect P10/P11 joints (rework evidence) + flex test the P5 link chain (the two link-pad joints in the P5→pin-1 rail chain are exactly what CV cannot vouch for). 5. At the range (next visit, before parts are ordered): XLO solenoid coils ≈1.7 kΩ and insulation to chassis; pot switch action + wiper sweep on both XLO pots; harness continuity terminal-to-terminal; connector pins clean and tight.

Powered bench (highest-value functional test; safety rules below): 6. VDD (IC1 pin 1↔20): 5 V and low AC ripple; pin 7 ≈VDD steady (no reset cycling); pins 3/4 oscillating. 7. Functional injection test: replicate one XLO position per the service-manual hookup — 10 kΩ pot from P5 to P14 and the pot's spark-switch contact wired as the harness does (the exact request node is open item Q2/Q4 — follow the PDR36/PRG wiring diagram rather than guessing); load P9–P10 with a real XLO solenoid or an equivalent ~1.7 kΩ/10 W dummy. Watch pins 16–19 switch and the solenoid clunk/cycle (~50 VAC-class drive). Interpret per §4.1 (false-pass/false-fail modes). 8. Relay contact drop P7→P4 while driving a real load.

Safety (upgraded per red-team): these are line-referenced circuits — P5/P14/VDD are NOT safe low voltage despite the "5 V" name. Isolation transformer and a fused/current-limited feed; one-hand rule; every fixture (pot, switch, dummy load) insulated, enclosed, and pre-wired — no touching anything while energized; NO grounded scope on line-referenced nodes (battery/diff-probe only); discharge all energy storage (the 2.2 µF/400 V red cap through ~1 kΩ, plus the electrolytics) after every power-down. This is a gas-appliance ignition controller: after reinstall, verify no gas smell at idle, clean ignition, and stable XLO cycling before leaving it in service.

6. Repair & hardening (when the culprit is found)

  • Replace 1999-vintage electrolytics as a set with 105 °C low-ESR parts; uprate any failed zener to ≥1 W; check R32 for thermal stress; reflow suspect link/terminal joints; re-coat disturbed conformal areas.
  • Line-side aging parts (red-team addition — these are the parts the fault tree actually points at): if the SERIES dropper C12 measures low, replace with safety-rated X2 film caps of the same value and class (capacitance loss here IS the classic transformerless-supply death); inspect/replace the MOV (V1) if surge history is suspected; if relay contacts show drop under load, replace K1 (AZ943 is current production, cheap); any burned resistor gets a flameproof like-for-like; clean and respect creepage distances when reworking.
  • Whole-range surge protector = cheap insurance either way.
  • Parallel path: FixYourBoard repairs this exact board (~$150, 2-yr warranty, root cause report); UpFix lists it too; used boards $50–480 (beware HA/HB pot-compatibility and same-age refurbs). Keep this board as donor/testbed if a used one goes in.

§11b — ST62T20C datasheet deep-dive (2026-07-30, research/MCU_USAGE_NOTES.md)

Documentation-verified facts (DS Rev 4 + AN392, PDFs archived in research/datasheets/): - Pin map confirmed exact (pins 8–15 = PB7→PB0, 16–19 = PA3→PA0, ADC on port B only, 20 mA sink on port A only). No net relabeling needed. - Every reset parks all I/O as inputs with ~45 µA pull-ups — triac gates cannot fire and spark windows shut. ANY reset-parked MCU reproduces the exact both-XLO-dead-everything-else-fine symptom. The reset CAUSE is bench-classifiable at pin 7: static low = LVD/brown-out; 0.4–25 ms sawtooth = watchdog reset loop; solid high = MCU running (fault is elsewhere: inputs/firmware/outputs). - Clock spec headroom: the −40/+125 °C grade is rated 8 MHz only at VDD ≥ 4.5 V — a 5 V dropper rail sagging 10 % is already out of spec. (Owner ask: read RES1's printed frequency; 4 MHz would triple the margin.) - OTP retention is the sleeper suspect: spec-minimum retention is 10 yr at 55 °C but falls to ~1–3 yr at 70–90 °C junction (DS Fig 55 class curve). In a hot range enclosure, spec-minimum bit-fade OVERLAPS the observed ~2-year failure cadence. The fault tree's "MCU dead = last resort" ranking is hereby PROMOTED to a co-equal branch: diagnosed by exclusion (pin 7 high + rail good + inputs good + no drive), remedied only by donor board + thermal relocation. - Rail-referencing contradiction exposed: AN392's transformerless-drive topology puts the MCU domain LINE-referenced (5 V referred to triac MT1), which conflicts with PB0 (pin 15) sitting directly on the P6/NEUT net if C12 is the series dropper. Three discriminating beeps added to the beep list (A3 P5<->P6; pin15<->P6; the N84 cluster <-> pin 20). Also new spec-grounded "MCU alive but refusing to fire" mechanisms: ADC source impedance <=10 kOhm and ripple limits — WORN POTS now have a concrete mechanism to silently suppress XLO.
